About Hydro ML Studio

Machine learning for water level, discharge & rainfall

Open Hydro ML Studio

What it does

Train and compare eight machine-learning models to simulate or forecast a hydrological series: automatic lag, rolling and seasonal features, cross-validation, Grid / Random / Bayesian tuning, NSE · KGE · PBIAS with Moriasi ratings, gap filling and Excel export.

  • SVR, random forest, extra trees, gradient boosting, neural network, KNN, tree, Lasso
  • Save & reload trained models
  • One-click zip of all results & plots
  • n-fold time-series cross-validation
  • Grid, random & Bayesian hyper-parameter tuning
  • Importance, partial dependence, duration curves
  • Recursive gap filling & prediction on new files
  • Methods paragraph + full Excel report

Problem

Train and compare machine-learning models to simulate or forecast water level, discharge or rainfall. Lag features, eight models (SVR, random forest, gradient boosting, neural network and more), cross-validation, automatic tuning and hydrology metrics.

Method

  • Simulate: inputs at the same time step are known, e.g. rebuilding a missing record from rainfall on the same day. Forecast: only data at least h steps old may be used, so the model predicts the target h steps ahead — the honest setting for early warning.
  • Lag analysis: for an input, cross-correlation with the target at each lag — peaks show the travel time. For the target itself: partial autocorrelation (PACF), the extra information each past value adds once the shorter lags are known. Computed on the training period only.
  • Splitting the record: the model learns from the training part and is judged on the test part it never saw. Keep the split chronological for time series: random rows let the model peek at the day before and after, which makes scores look better than they really are.
  • Which models? Start with the baselines (persistence, climatology): a useful model must beat them. Tree ensembles (random forest, extra trees, gradient boosting) and SVR are usually the strongest on hydrological data; linear regression is the easiest to explain. Train several and compare.
  • Cross-validation: repeats training and testing on different slices of the training period to see how stable a model is. Time-series: each fold trains on the past and tests on the next block (no peeking ahead). Blocked k-fold: the record is cut into k blocks and each block is tested once.
  • Search methods: Grid search tries every combination of the listed values — thorough but slow. Random search samples combinations at random — often as good with far fewer trials. Bayesian optimisation fits a Gaussian-process model of score vs settings and picks the next trial where improvement is most likely (expected improvement) — usually the fewest trials.
  • Performance was evaluated with the Nash–Sutcliffe efficiency (NSE; Nash and Sutcliffe, 1970), the Kling–Gupta efficiency (KGE; Gupta et al., 2009), the coefficient of determination (R²), RMSE, MAE, percent bias (PBIAS) and the RMSE–observations standard deviation ratio (RSR).
  • Importance methods: Built-in comes from the model itself (how much the trees’ splits on a feature reduce error, or standardized coefficients for linear models). Permutation shuffles one feature at a time on the test period and measures the drop in NSE — slower, but works for every model and is easier to compare.

Data

An .xlsx, .csv or .txt file with a date column and one or more numeric columns — for example daily rainfall, upstream water level and the downstream water level you want to predict. Dates and numbers are detected automatically. BWDB downloads list each day several times: REGULAR readings plus HIGH and LOW tide / peak readings; keeping only REGULAR rows gives one consistent reading per time step. 'Try an example' loads a built-in example marked 'EXAMPLE · SYNTHETIC DATA'.

Limitations

  • Ratings combine NSE, RSR and PBIAS thresholds of Moriasi et al. (2007), written for monthly streamflow — use them as a guide for other variables and time steps.
  • Linear regression: cannot capture thresholds or non-linear runoff on its own.
  • K-nearest neighbours: simple and assumption-free, but cannot predict beyond the range it has seen (e.g. a record flood) and slows down on long records.
  • Neural network (multi-layer perceptron): flexible and smooth but needs more data and is more sensitive to settings.
  • Models that use past target values fill gaps recursively: each predicted step feeds the next one.
