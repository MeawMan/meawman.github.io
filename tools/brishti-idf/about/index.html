About Brishti IDF

Brishti (বৃষ্টি) means rain in Bangla.

Rainfall IDF curves with every method under your control

Open Brishti IDF

What it does

Load a rainfall record (1-minute to daily, BMD daily sheets or annual-maximum tables) and build intensity–duration–frequency curves: quality checks, annual maxima or peaks over threshold, eight distributions fitted by L-moments, moments or maximum likelihood, IDF equations, short durations from daily data, design storms and a methods report. Every option has an explanation, and the numerics are tested against SciPy.

  • Gumbel, GEV, LP3, LN2/LN3, Gamma, P3, GLO; L-moments, moments or ML
  • GEV with a fixed (e.g. regional) shape; rank by AICc, BIC, A², PPCC or KS
  • Peaks over threshold with a threshold-stability table
  • Koutsoyiannis general IDF plus Talbot, Sherman, Bernard, Kimijima and more
  • Daily to sub-daily: IMD, calibrated power law, scaling, ratio transfer
  • Bootstrap confidence intervals, trend and outlier screening
  • Design storms for SWMM and HEC-HMS; report, Excel and methods paragraph

Problem

Rainfall intensity–duration–frequency (IDF) analysis that runs entirely in your web browser. Nothing is uploaded: the data never leave your computer. The tool was built with Bangladesh's data situation in mind (many daily gauges, few sub-daily ones), but works anywhere.

Method

  • Quality control: gaps, negative and implausible values, flat-lined sensors, long dry runs in wet months, record length, Mann–Kendall trend test, and a record-strip chart.
  • Annual maxima: sliding (rolling) windows for any durations (default 5 min to 48 h), calendar or water year, coverage rules, a fixed-interval (Hershfield) correction, and the empirical correction factor when sub-daily data exist.
  • Frequency analysis: Gumbel, GEV, Log-Pearson III, Log-Normal (2- and 3-parameter), Gamma, Pearson III and GLO, fitted by L-moments, moments or maximum likelihood (GEV also by L-moments with a fixed shape, and by penalised likelihood, experimental). Candidates ranked by AICc, BIC, Anderson–Darling, probability-plot correlation or KS; bootstrap confidence intervals; parametric-bootstrap p-values.
  • Peaks over threshold: independent storms above a threshold, generalised Pareto or exponential excesses (L-moments or ML), Poisson conversion to annual return periods, a threshold-stability table, and the option to use the result for design.
  • IDF equations: the Koutsoyiannis et al. (1998) general IDF (Gumbel/GEV; robust, one-step least-squares and likelihood estimation), plus Talbot, Bernard, Sherman, Kimijima, a general four-parameter form and three unified-in-T forms. Fitting uses multi-start and differential-evolution optimisation.
  • Short durations from daily data: IMD formula, calibrated power law, simple scaling, depth-ratio transfer from a nearby sub-daily station, your own ratio table, and Bell (experimental), shown side by side, with a one-click hold-out test on any station that has sub-daily data.
  • Adjustments: climate-change uplift (% or %/°C, optionally larger for short durations) and areal reduction, shown beside the baseline.
  • Design storms: alternating block, Chicago, SCS Type I/IA/II/III, Huff quartiles, triangular and uniform; export to CSV, SWMM (rain file and [TIMESERIES]) and HEC-HMS.

Data

Load a rainfall record (1-minute to daily, tipping-bucket logs, or annual-maximum tables). Missing values may be blank, NA, -999 or ***; trace (T) is read as 0. All sample files are synthetic (generated by tools/make_samples.js). The only real record shipped is Denver hourly rainfall (July, 1949–1990) from the R package extRemes (GPL): see data/third-party/README.md.

Bundled sample data

  • Hourly series, date-time + rainfall layout (synthetic)
  • BMD-style daily sheet, two stations (synthetic)
  • Annual-maximum table (synthetic)
  • Several stations in one file (synthetic)
  • Denver hourly, real (July only, 42 years), from the R package extRemes (GPL); in-app button 'Denver hourly, real (July only, 42 years)'
  • In-app button 'Synthetic 5-min record (30 years)' (built-in stochastic generator, not real observations)
  • In-app button 'Synthetic daily record (40 years)' (built-in stochastic generator, not real observations)

Validation

  • The numerical core is tested against SciPy (see docs/VALIDATION.md).
  • Generated by node tools/run_validation.js on 2026-11-03 with Node v22.23.4; reference values from SciPy 1.18.1 (tests/reference/make_reference.py) and from the published sources named per row. Result: 56 of 56 test cases passed; 363 of 363 recorded checks passed.
  • Methods that estimate sub-daily rainfall from daily data are not validated for Bangladesh.
  • Hold-out validation of daily-to-sub-daily methods: sub-daily data are hidden; only fixed calendar-day totals are used to estimate 24-h quantiles (with the 1.13 fixed-interval factor), which are then disaggregated. Denver is a single semi-arid, convective, mid-latitude station with July-only data — it is NOT representative of Bangladesh monsoon rainfall. The second dataset is synthetic. Neither establishes accuracy for Bangladesh; that needs BMD/BWDB sub-daily records.
  • Regional analysis (H, D, growth curve) is checked for internal identities only; it has not been cross-checked against the R package lmomRFA and is labelled EXPERIMENTAL in the tool.
  • The user interface is tested by a headless-browser smoke test (Chromium only).

Limitations

  • No Bangladeshi sub-daily truth was available. Every daily-to-sub-daily result is therefore labelled experimental or "accuracy not validated".
  • Bell (1969) coefficients and the Leclerc–Schaake ARF formula were recalled, not checked against the original papers (labelled EXPERIMENTAL).
  • Stationarity is assumed. A Mann–Kendall test (no correction for serial correlation) is reported, but there is no non-stationary IDF model.
  • Bootstrap percentile intervals under-cover. In the tool's own tests, nominal 90% intervals covered the truth about 80% of the time for 40-year records, and the screen says so.
  • Peaks over threshold (v0.2) uses a known threshold equal to the smallest selected peak and Poisson arrivals. There are no bootstrap intervals for POT quantiles, and when POT is used for design the Koutsoyiannis models are not fitted.
  • Outlier screening is the single Grubbs–Beck test of Bulletin 17B; one outlier can mask another. The multiple Grubbs–Beck test of Bulletin 17C is not implemented. Flags are never acted on automatically.
  • 1.13/1.04 are average factors. Papalexiou et al. (2016) show the correction is random from year to year. Where sub-daily data exist, use the empirical factor the tool reports.
  • Climate uplift is a simple multiplier (% or %/°C). It does not downscale GCMs. No Bangladesh-specific areal reduction factors are built in.
