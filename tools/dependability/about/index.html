About Dependability Analyzer

Dependable water level, flow & rainfall for reports

Open Dependability Analyzer

What it does

Rank a long record with the Weibull plotting position (or Gringorten, Cunnane, Hazen) and read off dependable low and high values at any P. Filter years and months, convert the datum (e.g. mPWD to mMSL), and export report-style figures, a dependability table, a methods paragraph and an Excel workbook.

  • Dependable low and high values at any P
  • One value per year or every value, whole series or by month
  • Year and month filters, incomplete-year warnings
  • Datum conversion, e.g. mPWD to mMSL
  • Daily, monthly or yearly data, incl. Year × Jan…Dec tables
  • Reads BWDB files: title rows, REGULAR rows, daily mean of readings
  • Report-style figures (PNG), dependability table, Excel and report text

Problem

Dependable water levels, flows and rainfall from long records. Filter years and months, convert the datum, rank with a plotting position, and take report-ready figures and tables into your report.

Method

  • Each ranked value gets a plotting position. Weibull (the default, as in the report workbook) is D = R × 100 / (n + 1); Gringorten, Cunnane and Hazen use D = (R − a) × 100 / (n + 1 − 2a) with a = 0.44, 0.4 and 0.5. Low side is ranked high to low, high side low to high.
  • The value at P is taken at rank ROUND(P/100 × (n + 1 − 2a) + a), which for Weibull is the report's ROUND(P/100 × (n+1)), or interpolated between ranks if you choose that.
  • With one value per year, "dependable value in the year" uses the same rule inside each year, then the years are ranked.
  • Low side: value equalled or exceeded P % of the time (dependable flow, dependable low level). High side: value not exceeded P % of the time (dependable high level).

Data

Excel (.xlsx) or CSV: daily, monthly or yearly values with a date column, Year and Month columns, or a Year × Jan…Dec table. BWDB files repeat readings as HIGH / LOW rows; keep only the regular ones. 'Try an example series' loads a made-up example series, not real measurements.
