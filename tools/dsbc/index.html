Bias-correct

Every method is calibrated on the period where observations and the historical run overlap, then applied with the same parameters to the future run. Methods are scored on years held out from calibration, and you can see how each one changes the model's projected climate signal.

Correct several variables jointly

Univariate methods give each variable the right distribution but leave the relationship between variables as the climate model made it. Multivariate methods also correct that relationship. The demo pairs daily rainfall with maximum temperature: at the station, rainy days are clearly cooler than dry days, but in the climate model they are barely cooler. A crop, drought or runoff model driven by both (temperature often enters through evaporation) then sees the wrong mix of hot-dry and cool-wet days, even when each input on its own looks right. Any variables can be chosen; pick them in the same order for all three series.
